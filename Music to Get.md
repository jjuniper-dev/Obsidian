If your goal is not simply “high-resolution music” but rather a curated reference collection that helps you appreciate what your system can do, then I’d look at sources audiophiles use to discover exceptional recordings, not just exceptional formats.

1.

Qobuz Magazine⁠

Probably the best starting point.

Qobuz regularly publishes:

- Best-recorded albums
- Essential jazz collections
- Audiophile reference recordings
- Hidden gems by genre
- Label spotlights

They focus heavily on recording quality and mastering quality.

2.

The Absolute Sound Super Disc List⁠

For decades, audiophiles have used the Super Disc List as a reference.

You won’t agree with every selection, but it is excellent for finding albums that reveal:

- Soundstage
- Imaging
- Dynamics
- Vocal realism
- Bass articulation

3.

Stereophile Records to Die For⁠

Each year writers nominate albums they would keep if they could only own a few.

The result is often more interesting than “audiophile demo tracks” because the music itself matters.

4. Reddit communities

- r/audiophile⁠
- r/headphones⁠
- r/audiophilemusic⁠

These communities frequently discuss:

- Best-mastered versions
- Specific pressings
- Reference tracks

The collective wisdom is often more useful than review magazines.

5. Record labels worth following

Some labels consistently produce exceptional recordings.

Jazz

- ECM Records
- Blue Note Records

Acoustic / Folk

- Chesky Records

Classical

- Reference Recordings
- BIS Records

Modern Audiophile

- 2L

2L is famous for producing recordings that many people use to evaluate new equipment.

A curated “James starter pack”

Knowing your interests—systems thinking, detail, spatial reasoning, AI, geospatial intelligence, and appreciation for technical craftsmanship—I suspect you’d get more enjoyment from recordings with:

- Large spatial environments
- Layered arrangements
- Exceptional imaging
- Real acoustic spaces

I’d start with:

- Friday Night in San Francisco
- Jazz at the Pawnshop
- Mule Variations
- The Köln Concert
- Random Access Memories
- Aja
- Brothers in Arms
- Tales of Mystery and Imagination

These are albums people return to for decades because they’re both musically compelling and technically revealing.

A different approach

Instead of trying to build a library of 500 albums, build a reference library of 25–50 albums across categories:

- Female vocal
- Male vocal
- Jazz trio
- Small acoustic ensemble
- Large orchestra
- Electronic
- Rock
- Live recording
- Folk
- Experimental

That gives you a permanent collection you can use to evaluate every future upgrade—whether it’s your PS200 Pro, a future DAC, speakers, headphones, or even room treatments. It’s the audio equivalent of maintaining a trusted benchmark dataset in an AI platform.

You can:

1. Create a free Qobuz account.
2. Buy albums individually.
3. Download the FLAC files (CD quality or Hi-Res if available).

In fact, Qobuz explicitly states that you do not need a subscription to purchase and download music. The subscription mainly adds streaming access and discounts on purchases.  

For your use case, I’d do this:

|   |   |
|---|---|
|Need|Solution|
|Casual listening|Apple Music|
|Favorite 25 albums|Buy from Qobuz|
|Bit-perfect playback|UAPP + local FLAC files|
|Mobile listening|Apple Music|
|Long-term ownership|FLAC files stored in PCA/OneDrive/NAS|

The nice part is that once you’ve bought the album, you can download it as FLAC and play it with:

- USB Audio Player PRO
- Foobar2000
- MusicBee
- Jellyfin
- Navidrome
- Roon
- Any other FLAC-capable player

No ongoing Qobuz payment required.  

There is one thing I’d recommend: always keep your own backup copy.

Qobuz allows re-downloads of purchased music, but the audiophile community generally treats the downloaded FLAC as the thing you own and backs it up locally rather than relying on Qobuz as permanent cloud storage.  

For PCA, I would literally create:

OneDrive

└── Media

    └── Music

        ├── Reference Albums

        ├── Test Tracks

        └── Hi-Res

Then sync that folder to:

- PC
- MacBook
- Pixel (selected albums)

That’s probably the highest-value, lowest-cost path for someone who already gets Apple Music bundled and only cares deeply about a relatively small curated collection.
