# ISO/IEC 22989 — Lifecycle Concepts

*Source: ISO/IEC 22989:2022 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Concepts and terminology*

## AI Lifecycle

The complete sequence of phases through which an AI system passes: problem definition, data collection, model design and training, validation, deployment, operation, monitoring, and retirement.

**Related:** Training, Inference, Deployment, Model evaluation

---

## Training (Model Training)

The iterative process of adjusting an AI model's parameters by minimizing a loss function over training data, enabling the model to learn the patterns necessary to perform its intended task.

**Related:** Training data, Fine-tuning, Overfitting, AI lifecycle

---

## Fine-tuning

Additional training of a pre-trained model on a smaller domain-specific dataset to adapt its capabilities to a new task or context while retaining general knowledge.

**Related:** Transfer learning, Foundation model, Training

---

## Transfer Learning

A technique that applies knowledge gained while training on one task or dataset to improve performance on a related but different task or domain, typically requiring less data and compute.

**Related:** Fine-tuning, Foundation model, AI model

---

## Inference

The process of applying a trained AI model to new, unseen input data to produce predictions, classifications, recommendations, or generated outputs during deployment.

**Related:** AI model, Deployment, AI system

---

## Deployment

The operationalization of a trained AI model within a production environment where it processes real-world inputs and delivers outputs to users or downstream systems.

**Related:** Inference, AI lifecycle, Monitoring

---

## Monitoring

The ongoing observation and assessment of an AI system's behavior and performance in production to detect data drift, performance degradation, unexpected outputs, or safety issues.

**Related:** Deployment, AI lifecycle, Reliability, Robustness

---

## Model Evaluation

The systematic measurement of an AI model's performance against defined metrics—such as accuracy, precision, recall, F1, or BLEU—on held-out test data or through human evaluation.

**Related:** Test data, Validation data, Ground truth

---

## Overfitting

A condition in which a model learns the training data too precisely—including noise and irrelevant patterns—resulting in poor generalization to new, unseen data.

**Related:** Training data, Validation data, Regularization

---

## Supervised Learning

A machine learning paradigm in which a model is trained on labeled data—input-output pairs—with the objective of learning a mapping from inputs to outputs that generalizes to unseen examples.

**Related:** Labeling, Training data, Unsupervised learning

---

## Unsupervised Learning

A machine learning paradigm in which a model identifies patterns, structures, or clusters in unlabeled data without explicit target outputs provided during training.

**Related:** Supervised learning, Training data

---

## Reinforcement Learning

A machine learning paradigm in which an agent learns to take actions in an environment to maximize a cumulative reward signal, improving its policy through trial and error.

**Related:** Machine learning, AI model
