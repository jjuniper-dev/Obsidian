# ISO/IEC 22989 — Trustworthy AI Properties

*Source: ISO/IEC 22989:2022 — Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Concepts and terminology*

## Explainability

The degree to which the rationale behind an AI system's output—predictions, decisions, or recommendations—can be presented in human-understandable terms to support audit, oversight, or user trust.

**Related:** Interpretability, Transparency, Accountability

---

## Interpretability

The extent to which the internal mechanisms of an AI model can be understood and described in meaningful terms, enabling users and developers to comprehend how inputs are transformed into outputs.

**Related:** Explainability, Transparency

---

## Transparency

The property of an AI system or the conduct of AI actors whereby information about the system—its purpose, design, data, capabilities, limitations, and decision logic—is available and accessible to relevant stakeholders.

**Related:** Explainability, Accountability, Traceability

---

## Robustness

The ability of an AI system to maintain performance within acceptable bounds when encountering adversarial inputs, distribution shift, unexpected conditions, or noisy data.

**Related:** Reliability, Adversarial attack, Safety

---

## Reliability

The ability of an AI system to perform its intended functions consistently and predictably under defined conditions over a specified period, meeting performance specifications across operational contexts.

**Related:** Robustness, Safety, Monitoring

---

## Safety

The property of an AI system that ensures it does not cause unacceptable harm to persons, organizations, or the environment during its intended operation or foreseeable misuse.

**Related:** Reliability, Robustness, Human-in-the-loop

---

## Security

The resistance of an AI system to unauthorized access, adversarial manipulation, data poisoning, model inversion, and other deliberate attempts to compromise its confidentiality, integrity, or availability.

**Related:** Robustness, Adversarial attack, Privacy

---

## Privacy

The protection of personal data and sensitive information throughout the AI lifecycle—collection, training, inference, and storage—ensuring individuals' rights to data control and confidentiality.

**Related:** Security, Synthetic data, Data quality

---

## Fairness

The absence of unjustified differential treatment in AI system outcomes across demographic groups or individuals, assessed against agreed criteria of equity, non-discrimination, and equitable impact.

**Related:** Bias (Data), Accountability, Affected party

---

## Accountability

The obligation of AI actors—developers, operators, and deployers—to justify their decisions regarding AI systems, accept responsibility for outcomes, and provide mechanisms for redress when harm occurs.

**Related:** Transparency, Explainability, Human-in-the-loop

---

## Traceability

The ability to track and document the provenance of data, model versions, design decisions, and outputs across the AI lifecycle to support audit, reproducibility, and accountability.

**Related:** Transparency, Accountability, AI lifecycle

---

## Resilience

The capacity of an AI system to withstand, adapt to, and recover from disruptions—such as infrastructure failures, distributional shifts, or adversarial conditions—while maintaining acceptable performance.

**Related:** Robustness, Reliability, Safety
